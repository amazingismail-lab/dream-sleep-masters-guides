Side Sleeping Rounded Shoulders: What the Evidence Shows

By Daniel Sebastian Montgomery · September 2026

If you wake up feeling hunched, it's natural to blame your favorite side. But the research on side sleeping rounded shoulders is thinner than most sleep advice suggests, and the fixable part is usually how your top arm and neck are positioned, not the side itself. Does side sleeping actually cause rounded shoulders?

There's no direct study confirming that it does. A 2019 scoping review searched thousands of papers and found only four that looked at sleep posture and spinal symptoms at all, and none measured shoulder rounding specifically. Where the review did find a pattern, side lying came out "generally protective" against waking spinal symptoms, which suggests the position itself isn't the problem — how you arrange your arm and neck within it may matter more.

What actually causes rounded shoulders?

Mostly daytime habits, based on the data that exists. A 2023 study comparing more and less physically active young adults found the less active group had noticeably more shoulder rounding, tied to a tighter pectoralis minor muscle. A 2017 review also linked rounded shoulders to a more curved upper back, noting the two often show up together rather than alone.

Could a sleep position still make it worse overnight?

It's plausible, even without a study proving it. If your top arm falls unsupported across your chest while you sleep, the shoulder blade can get pulled forward with it, holding the muscles in a shortened position for hours. A 2010 hypothesis paper also raised the idea that sustained pressure on the bottom shoulder from lying on it could contribute to shoulder pain, and a 2012 study of chiropractic patients found 67% of one-sided sleepers favored their already-painful shoulder — though the authors were careful to say they couldn't tell which came first.

Note

A 2021 study using infrared cameras filmed 53 sleepers at home and found people with morning neck pain spent over twice as long in postures the researchers classed as "provocative." It's a small, observational study, so treat it as a clue rather than proof.

How tall should your pillow be?

Tall enough to keep your neck roughly level with the rest of your spine, and your shoulder width is the main factor. A 2025 biomechanics study tested pillow heights from 8 to 14 cm and found shoulder width predicted which height kept the neck best aligned — broader shoulders generally needed more loft. This is the logic behind firmer, taller pillows like the FirmNest Extra Firm Pillow: a soft pillow that compresses under the head can undo the height it started with.

Does hugging a pillow help your shoulders?

It may, according to physical therapists who work with shoulder pain. For side sleepers dealing with a sore shoulder, a Cleveland Clinic physical therapist recommends building a "pillow wall" in front of the chest and resting the top arm on it, roughly the height of the body, so the arm isn't left to drop.

Tip

A body pillow can double as both an arm rest and a knee pillow, which is easier to keep in place overnight than two separate pillows that shift as you move.

Is a knee pillow worth adding too?

Probably, if your hips tend to twist. According to the Sleep Foundation, an unsupported top leg can fall forward and pull the lower back out of alignment, which lines up with the "provocative" twisted side-lying posture noted in the 2021 camera study above.

Can exercise actually fix rounded shoulders?

There's more evidence for this than for any pillow change. A 2024 meta-analysis of 22 studies found that targeted stretching and strengthening improved rounded-shoulder angles in people with upper crossed syndrome. A supported sleep setup can stop the night from working against that progress, but the posture correction itself appears to happen while you're awake.

When should you see a doctor about it?

If rounding bothers you during the day, or if you have shoulder or neck pain that persists for more than a couple of weeks despite a better sleep setup, it's worth checking with a doctor or physical therapist rather than adjusting pillows further. Frequently asked questions

Does side sleeping cause rounded shoulders?

No study has directly tested this. A 2019 scoping review found only four studies on sleep posture and spinal symptoms, and side lying was generally protective against waking symptoms. Rounded shoulders are more consistently linked to daytime factors like low activity, though an unsupported top arm may hold the shoulder forward overnight.

What's the best pillow height for side sleepers?

It depends on your shoulder width. A 2025 study testing heights from 8 to 14 cm found shoulder width predicted which height kept the neck best aligned, with broader shoulders generally needing more loft. A pillow that's firm enough to hold its shape matters as much as the height itself.

Does a body pillow help with shoulder posture?

It may help by giving the top arm somewhere to rest instead of dropping across the chest. Physical therapists sometimes recommend a "pillow wall" in front of the body for side sleepers with shoulder pain. A body pillow can also double as knee support in the same setup.

Should I put a pillow between my knees?

It can help keep your hips from twisting. Without support, the top leg may fall forward and pull the lower back out of alignment, according to sleep researchers. A dedicated knee pillow or a long body pillow both work for this.

Can I fix rounded shoulders through sleep alone?

Unlikely on its own. The strongest evidence for improving rounded-shoulder angles comes from a 2024 meta-analysis of exercise programs, not sleep changes. A supported side-sleeping setup may stop the position from making things worse, but stretching and strengthening during the day appear to do the actual correcting.
